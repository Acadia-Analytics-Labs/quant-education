# Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities (TIPS)

## Introduction

When investors buy bonds, one of the biggest risks they face is inflation. While a bond may promise a fixed interest payment, rising prices can reduce the purchasing power of those payments over time. For example, if an investor earns a 4% return on a bond but inflation rises to 5%, the investor’s real return (adjusted for inflation) is negative. 

To address this problem, the U.S. Treasury created Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities (TIPS). TIPS are a type of government bond designed to protect investors from inflation. 

## What are TIPS?

Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities are bonds issued by the U.S. Department of the Treasury that adjusts their value based on the changes in the Consumer Price Index (CPI), a measure of inflation.

Unlike traditional Treasury bonds, where the principal value (the amount of money that is borrowed and repaid at maturity) stays fixed, the principal of a TIPS bond rises when inflation increases and falls when inflation decreases. 

The key feature of TIPS is that investors receive protection against a decline in purchasing power. 

## How Do TIPS Work?

TIPS have three main components:

### 1\. Inflation-Adjusted Principal

When an investor buys a TIPS bond, they receive an initial principal amount. This principal is adjusted over time based on changes in the CPI.

If inflation increases, the principal value rises, and if prices fall (deflation), the principal value decreases. 

For example, suppose an investor buys $1,000 of TIPS. If inflation rises by 3%, the adjusted principal increases to:  
	  
$$1{,}000 \\times (1 \+ 0.03) \= 1{,}030$$

The investor now earns interest based on the higher $1,030 principal. 

### 2\. Fixed Interest Rate

TIPS pays a fixed interest rate, known as the coupon rate. However, because the principal changes with inflation, the actual dollar amount of interest payments changes as well.

For example for a bond with an initial principal of $1,000 and a coupon rate of 2%

The first interest payment would be:

$$1{,}000 \\times 0.02 \= 20$$

After 3% inflation:

$$1{,}030 \\times 0.02 \= 20.60$$

As inflation increases the principal, interest payments also rise. 

### 3\. Principal Protection at Maturity

When the bond matures, investors receive the inflation-adjusted principal. If deflation causes the principal to fall below the original amount, the Treasury guarantees that investors receive at least the original face value. 

This provides a floor that protects investors from losing principal due to deflation. 

## TIPS Yields

The yield on TIPS is called the real yield because it represents the return an investor receives after accounting for inflation. 

For example:

* A traditional 10-year Treasury bond yields 4.5%  
* A 10-year TIPS bond yields 2.0%

The difference between the yields is known as the breakeven inflation rate. 

$$\\text{Breakeven Inflation Rate} \= \\text{Nominal Treasury Yield} \- \\text{TIPS Real Yield}$$

So for this example the breakeven inflation rate would be:

$$4.5\\% \- 2.0\\% \= 2.5\\%$$

The market is implying that investors expect inflation to average about 2.5% over the next 10 years. If actual inflation is higher than the breakeven rate, TIPS will outperform traditional Treasury bonds. If inflation is lower, traditional Treasury bonds will perform better. 

## Why Do Investors Buy TIPS?

Investors typically buy TIPS for three main reasons;

### Inflation Protection

Tips helps preserve purchasing power during periods of rising prices. This makes them attractive during periods of high or uncertain inflation.

### Portfolio Diversification

TIPS can behave differently from stocks and traditional bonds. Adding them to a portfolio can reduce exposure to inflation risk. 

### Retirement Planning

Many retirees rely on fixed income investments. Since inflation can reduce the value of fixed payments over time, TIPS can help protect long-term purchasing power. 

## Risks of Investing in TIPS

Although TIPS provide inflation protection, they are not risk-free.

### Interest Rate Risk

Just like traditional bonds, TIPS prices can fall when interest rates rise. Even though the principal adjusts for inflation, investors who sell the bond before maturity may receive less than they paid for it. 

### Deflation Risk

While the Treasury guarantees the original principal at maturity, periods of sustained deflation can reduce the adjusted principal and interest payments during the life of the bond. 

### Inflation Measurement Risk

TIPS are linked to the CPI, which may not perfectly reflect an individual investor’s personal inflation experience. For example, healthcare or housing costs may rise faster than the overall CPI. 

## TIPS in Monetary Policy

TIPS are also important because they provide insight into market expectations for inflation. The difference between Treasury yields and TIPS yields help economists and policymakers estimate how much inflation investors expect in the future. 

The Federal Reserve closely monitors these inflation expectations because they influence decisions about interest rates and monetary policy. 

If inflation expectations rise significantly, the Fed may respond by raising interest rates to slow demand and prevent inflation from becoming persistent. 

## Conclusion

Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities are a unique type of government bond design to protect investors from inflation. By adjusting their principal value based on changes in the Consumer Price Index, TIPS help preserve purchasing power and provide investors with a way to hedge against rising prices.

While TIPS are not guaranteed to outperform traditional bonds, they are an important tool for investors managing inflation risk and for economists studying market expectations about future inflation.
