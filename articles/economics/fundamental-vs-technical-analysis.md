# Fundamental Analysis vs Technical Analysis

Fundamental analysis and technical analysis are two of the most common approaches that investors use to evaluate stocks. The main difference between the two is what they believe drives stock prices. Fundamental analysis seeks to answer the question, “Is the company worth buying?” while technical analysis focuses on “When is the best time to buy or sell?”

## Fundamental Analysis

Fundamental analysis is the process of evaluating a stock by determining the company's intrinsic value. This is done by examining its financial health, competitive position, and future growth prospects. Investors then compare the intrinsic value to the current market price to determine whether the stock is overvalued or undervalued. 

Investors that use fundamental analysis might examine: 

* **Financial Statements**: revenue, earnings, margins, debt levels, and cash flow from the income statement, balance sheet, and cash flow statement  
* **Valuation Ratios**: price-to-earnings (P/E), price-to-book (P/B), enterprise value-to-EBITDA (EV/EBITDA), discounted cash flow (DCF) models   
* **Business Fundamentals**: competitive advantages, management quality, industry trends, regulatory environment, etc.   
* **Macroeconomic Factors**: interest rates, inflation, GDP growth, consumer spending, and how they affect the company's sector 

### Core Belief 

Fundamental analysis is based on the idea that every company has an intrinsic value that is determined by its underlying business performance. Although market prices can vary from that value in the short-run, the belief is that they will move towards the intrinsic value over time. This means that fundamental analysis tends to operate on a longer time horizon (months to years) since it can take time for a market to recognize a mispricing. 

Famous investors such as Benjamin Graham and Warren Buffet built their investment philosophies based on fundamental analysis by buying assets for less than they are worth. 

### Example 

Suppose Company A is trading at $40 per share.

After analyzing the company's financial statements, competitive position, and future earnings, an investor estimates the company's intrinsic value of $60 per share. 

Since the market price is lower than the estimated intrinsic value, the investor may determine that the stock is undervalued. If the investor purchases the stock it is because they believe that the price will eventually move closer to its true value, which would give the investor a positive return. 

### Advantages

* Identifies long-term investment opportunities  
* Helps investors to understand the business  
* Not influenced as much by short-term market volatility  
* Useful for value investing and long-term management

### Disadvantages

* Time consuming and research intensive  
* Intrinsic value estimates can depend on assumptions   
* Market prices can sometimes remain disconnected from intrinsic values for long periods 

## Technical Analysis 

Technical analysis assumes that all publicly available information is already reflected in a stock’s price. Instead of studying the company itself, investors will examine historical price movements and trading volume to identify patterns that may indicate future price behavior. 

Investors that use technical analysis might examine: 

* **Price Charts**: candlestick charts, line charts, and bar charts are used to visualize price action over time   
* **Volume Analysis**: trading activity and breakout confirmation that are backed by high trading volume are generally considered to be more meaningful  
* **Indicators**: moving averages, the Relative Strength Index (RSI), and Moving average Convergence Divergence (MACD) help to quantify momentum and trend strength

### Core Beliefs 

Technical analysis is built on three widely accepted principles.

**Price Discounts Everything**  
All known information (fundamentals, news, and sentiment) are all already reflected in the current price, so there is no need to analyze it separately. As a result technical analysts focus on price behavior rather than analyzing the underlying business. 

**Price Moves in Trends**  
Once a trend is established (upward, downward, or sideways), it is more likely to continue than to reverse immediately. 

**History Tends to Repeat Itself**  
Investor psychology and market behavior is consistent over time and often follows recurring patterns. This means recognizable chart patterns tend to produce similar outcomes.

Technical analysis is most commonly used by traders with shorter investment horizons, ranging from minutes to hours or several weeks. 

### Example 

A stock has traded between $95 and $100 several times over the past six months. 

One day, it rises above $100 with unusually high trading volume. 

A technical analyst may interpret this as a breakout, suggesting that buying pressure is increasing and the stock could continue rising.

### Advantages

* Helps to identify potential entry and exit points   
* Useful for short-term trading   
* Provides objective trading signals  
* Can be applied to any liquid financial asset

### Disadvantages

* Does not assess a company's financial strength   
* Chart patterns are not always reliable   
* Different analysts may interpret the same chart differently   
* Can produce false signals during volatile markets conditions 

## Which Approach Should You Use?

Both approaches have many pros and cons because they serve different purposes. 

Fundamental analysis is generally preferred by long-term investors who want to identify companies trading below their intrinsic value. Technical analysis is more commonly used by short-term investors who seek to profit from price movements and market trends. 

Most professional investors combine both of these approaches. A portfolio manager might use fundamental analysis to identify undervalued companies with strong long-term prospects, then use technical analysis to choose favorable entry and exit points based on market trends and price momentum. This combination allows investors to answer both what to buy and when to buy or sell.
