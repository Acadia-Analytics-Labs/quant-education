# The Sticker Price Is a Little Fib 🏷️

You spot a shiny robot toy. The tag says **\$20**. So it costs \$20, right?

Not really. By the time it's actually yours, you paid *more* than \$20 — and if you ever sell it back, you'll get *way less* than \$20. The number on the tag is more like a rumor than a promise.

Grown-up traders have the exact same problem when they buy and sell stocks. The price you see flashing on the screen is almost never the price you really get. The little differences look tiny, but they add up and quietly eat your money.

## The tag price and the real price are different

Three sneaky things happen between the tag and your wallet:

- 🧾 **Extra fees get added** (like sales tax). In trading, this is the **commission** — a small fee for making the trade.
- 🏃 **The price moves while you decide.** The cheap ones sell out and you pay more than you planned. Traders call this **slippage** — getting a worse price than you expected.
- ↔️ **Buying and selling aren't the same price.** A store sells the toy for \$23 but only buys it back for \$12. Markets do this too: the price to *buy* is always a little higher than the price to *sell*.


```ascii
   Store SELLS to you at ........ $23   (you buy HIGH)
   Store BUYS from you at ....... $12   (you sell LOW)
   ------------------------------------------------
   The gap in the middle = the store's hidden cut
```


That gap between "buy high" and "sell low" has a name: the **spread**. Every time you buy something and later sell it, you have to cross that gap — and crossing it costs you.

## Why this matters

Imagine a game where you're *really* good and win a little bit every round. But every round, a tiny coin drops out of your pocket without you noticing. Play once, no big deal. Play a thousand times, and all those tiny coins could add up to more than you won!

That's exactly what happens to traders who buy and sell a lot. Their clever plan looks great on paper — but after all the spreads, fees, and slippage, the real profit is much smaller. Sometimes it disappears completely.

So the big-kid rule is simple: **always ask what you'll really pay, not what the tag says.** The tag is a fib. Your wallet knows the truth. 💡
