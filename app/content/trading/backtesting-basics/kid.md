# Don't Peek at the Answer Key! 📝

Imagine you have a big spelling test tomorrow. Your teacher gives you a **practice sheet** to study. Great! But there's a trick: the answer key is sitting *right there* on the desk.

If you copy the answers onto your practice sheet, you'll get a perfect score. It feels amazing. But did you actually *learn* to spell? Nope. You just fooled yourself. The real test — with **new words you've never seen** — will tell the truth.

Grown-up traders do the same thing with old stock-market data. They test a money-making idea on the past to see if it *would have* worked. That's called a **backtest**. And just like the practice sheet, it's super easy to peek at the answers and trick yourself.

## The big idea in one sentence

> **If you can peek at the answers, a perfect score means nothing.**

A backtest is a "practice test" for a trading idea, using the past as the practice sheet. The honest way to grade it is to use market data your idea has **never seen** — that's called *out-of-sample* testing. It's the real quiz.

## Four sneaky ways people fool themselves

- 👀 **Peeking at the answer key.** Using a fact from *tomorrow* to make *today's* bet — like knowing Friday's weather when packing lunch on Monday. In real life you never get that peek.
- 🏆 **Only counting the winners.** Imagine judging a video game as "easy" by only asking the kids who *beat* it. The ones who lost and quit aren't in the room! Old lists of "top companies" quietly drop the ones that went bust.
- 🎯 **Memorizing instead of learning.** If you tweak your rule until it nails every single practice question *exactly*, you memorized the practice sheet — not the skill. New questions crush you.
- 🍿 **Forgetting the price of playing.** Every trade costs a little money, like an arcade charging a token per game. Ignore those tokens and your "winnings" are a mirage.

So the whole trick to a good backtest is the same trick as an honest quiz: **test yourself on things you've never seen, and don't peek.** 🌱✅
