# The Market's Dashboard 🚗

When you ride in a car, the dashboard has a bunch of little dials called **gauges**. One shows how **fast** you're going. One shows how much **gas** is left. One shows if the engine is getting too **hot**. Each gauge tells you exactly *one* thing — and a good driver glances at all of them before deciding what to do.

The stock market has gauges too. Traders call them **indicators**: little meters that read the price and each report one honest fact about it. This is your cheat sheet to the most common ones.

## The three big gauges

Every trading indicator is really one of these three car gauges in disguise:

- 🏎️ **Speed gauge → momentum.** Is the price speeding up or slowing down? *Momentum* just means how fast something is moving. (Real names: **RSI**, **MACD**.)
- ⛽ **Fuel / direction gauge → trend.** Which way are we headed — up or down? *Trend* means the general direction. (Real names: **moving averages** like SMA and EMA, which are just a smoothed-out price.)
- 🌡️ **Temperature gauge → volatility.** How bumpy and wild is the ride right now? *Volatility* means how big the swings are. (Real names: **Bollinger Bands**, **ATR**.)

```ascii
   |   [ SPEED ]     [ FUEL ]     [ TEMP ]     |

   +-------------------------------------------+
        one gauge each — read them together
```


## The golden rule

**No single gauge tells you the whole story.** If you only watch the speedometer, you might run out of gas. If you only watch the fuel, you might crash from going too fast. A great driver takes a quick glance at *all* of them and then decides.

So an indicator isn't a magic crystal ball that predicts the future. It's just one honest little meter that measures one thing about the market. Read a few of them together, and you get a clear picture of the road ahead. 🧭
