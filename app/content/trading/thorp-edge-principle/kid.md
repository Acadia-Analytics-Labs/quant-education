Ever notice how a casino always seems to end up rich, even though people win jackpots there all the time? The casino isn't lucky. It has a **tiny secret advantage** on every little bet — and it plays *millions* of little bets. Ed Thorp, a math professor, figured out this trick and used it to beat blackjack and then Wall Street.

## The tiny-edge trick

An **edge** just means you win a little more often than you lose. Not always — just *a little* more. The magic is what happens when you use that edge over and over:

- Play **once** with a tiny edge → it's almost a coin flip. You might lose.
- Play **a thousand times** → the tiny edge shows up every single round and quietly stacks up. Now it's almost impossible to end up behind.

That's the **law of large numbers**: the more times you play a game with an edge, the more sure you are to come out ahead. One bet is luck. A thousand bets is math.

## Why not just bet it all?

Because one unlucky roll would knock you out of the game — and you can't win if you can't keep playing. So the casino (and smart traders) keep every bet **small**, on purpose. That way no single loss can end the story. (That's the whole idea of the **Position Sizing** lesson: bet small enough to survive a bad streak.)

So here's Thorp's big lesson in one line:

> **If you truly have an edge, you don't want *bigger* bets. You want *more* of them.**

Find a small advantage, keep each bet tiny, and let it play out hundreds of times. Be the casino — patient, boring, and almost impossible to beat. 🎰🏆
