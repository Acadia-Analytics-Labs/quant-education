# One Basket, Every Fruit 🧺

Say you get **\$50** and you want to *own some fruit*. You could march up to the stand and buy **one shiny apple**. But what if that one apple has a worm inside? Now your whole \$50 is a soggy mess. 🐛

Here's the smarter move: buy a **whole fruit basket** in a single purchase — apples, bananas, grapes, oranges, all at once. If one banana turns out bruised, who cares? You've still got a whole basket of yummy stuff.

An **ETF** (that stands for *exchange-traded fund*, a fancy grown-up name) is exactly that basket — but for companies instead of fruit. Buy **one** ETF and you quietly own a tiny slice of *hundreds* of companies at the same time.

## Why one basket beats one apple

When you own just **one** company, everything depends on that one company having a good day. That's a lot of pressure on a single apple! Spread your money across **many** companies and no single bad one can wipe you out. Grown-ups call this **diversification** — a big word that just means *don't put all your eggs (or apples) in one basket*.


```ascii
   ONE apple:      🍎            -> worm = total disaster
   ONE basket:  🍎🍌🍇🍊🍐🥝     -> one bad fruit = barely notice
```


## The tiny fee

Somebody has to pack the basket and keep it fresh, so the fund keeps a **teeny** slice each year — often just a few pennies for every \$100. That slice is called the **expense ratio**. The smaller it is, the more fruit stays yours. Good baskets keep it super small.

## The best part

You don't need to be rich or clever to pick winners. With **one** purchase you get a little piece of a whole crowd of companies — cheap, easy, and all at once. One ticket, the whole orchard. 🌍🧺
