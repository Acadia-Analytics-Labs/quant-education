# Bell Curves and Giant Waves 🌊

Line up your whole class and measure everyone's height. Most kids are pretty close to the same size. A few are a little taller, a few a little shorter, and almost nobody is a **giant** or **tiny as a mouse**.

If you draw that as a picture, it makes a hill shape called a **bell curve** — tall in the middle where most kids are, and low on both ends where the rare sizes are.

That picture has a fancy name: a **distribution**. A distribution just answers one question:

> **"How likely is each size of outcome?"**

## Two kinds of pictures

Heights make a **calm** distribution. The tallest kid isn't twenty feet tall — sizes stay in a sensible range. The bell curve fits great.

Waves make a **wild** distribution. Almost every wave is small, but the rare monster wave is *enormous* — way bigger than anything you'd guess from the calm days. Those rare monsters live in the **tails**, the far edges of the picture.

## Why traders care

When prices go up and down, people used to think it was calm like heights — a neat bell curve. But the market is really more like the beach. It has **surprise giant waves**: crash days and rocket days that are far bigger, and happen far more often, than the bell curve promises.

```ascii
  Calm (heights):     .-'''-.        <- bell curve, thin edges
                    -'       '-

  Wild (markets):    .-'''-.
                   -'       '--___    <- FAT tail: rare giants hide here
```

So the big idea is simple:

- A **distribution** is a picture of how likely each size of outcome is.
- Calm things (like heights) fit a tidy **bell curve**.
- Wild things (like markets) have **fat tails** — giant surprises show up more than the bell curve expects.

If you plan only for calm days, the giant wave will knock you over. Smart traders always remember the beach. 🌊🧠
