# Price Is Not the Same as Worth 🃏

Imagine you own a super-rare trading card. Its **real worth** is decided by how good it is — how strong the character is, how few of them exist, how useful it is in the game. That real worth barely changes from week to week.

But the **price** — what kids will actually pay you for it today — bounces all over the place. Why? Because price follows the **mood** of the crowd, and moods swing fast.

## The magnet trick

A grown-up investor named **Howard Marks** explains it like this: real worth acts like a **magnet**. The price can wander far away for a while — floating up in a hype **bubble**, or sinking down in a boring **crash** — but sooner or later it gets tugged back toward true worth.

```ascii
  price
   |     🎈 bubble (too excited)
   |    /  \
   |   /    \___   • • • • • • •  <- true worth (the magnet line)
   |  /         \      /
   | /           \____/  🕳️ bargain (too bored)
   +------------------------------> time
```

## Why this matters

The mistake is paying a **big** price when everyone is hyped. If you buy the card at the top of the bubble, you paid way more than it's really worth — and when the mood cools, you're stuck.

The smart move is the opposite: notice when the crowd is bored and the price has dropped **below** true worth. That's when a great thing is on sale.

So remember two things:
- 🎈 Excited crowd → price floats **too high**. Be careful.
- 🕳️ Bored crowd → price sinks **too low**. Look for bargains.

Price is what you pay today. Worth is what you actually get. Don't mix them up. 🧲
