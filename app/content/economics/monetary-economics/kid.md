# The Economy Has a Thermostat 🌡️

Every time you buy a snack, you hand over money — little tokens that everyone agrees are worth something. You can't eat a \$5 bill, but the store takes it happily, because tomorrow they can trade that same bill for something *they* want. That's the whole magic of money: it's the **tokens the whole world trades with**.

But who makes sure there's the *right amount* of tokens floating around? That's the job of one very special bank — the **central bank**. In the United States it's called the **Federal Reserve**, or just "the Fed." Think of it as the grown-up who controls the thermostat for the entire economy.

## Too hot or too cold?

Picture the economy as a giant room full of people buying and selling.

- 🔥 **Too hot:** everyone is spending like crazy, so shops keep raising prices. Your \$5 buys less than it used to. Grown-ups call rising prices **inflation**.
- 🥶 **Too cold:** nobody is buying, shops can't sell their stuff, and people start losing their jobs.

The central bank's job is to keep the room *just right* — comfortable, not boiling, not freezing.

## The dial it turns: interest rates

The thermostat's dial is the **interest rate** — basically the "rent" you pay to borrow money.

- To **cool things down**, the central bank **raises** the rate. Borrowing gets expensive, so people spend less and prices stop shooting up. 🧊
- To **warm things up**, it **lowers** the rate. Borrowing gets cheap, so people spend more, shops sell more, and jobs come back. ☀️
