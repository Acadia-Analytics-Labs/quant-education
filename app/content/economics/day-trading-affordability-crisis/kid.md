# Why Skipping the Line Usually Doesn't Work 🏠

Imagine everyone wants a treehouse of their very own. But the treehouses keep getting more and more expensive — faster than grown-ups can save their allowance. The line to buy one feels *endless*. So some grown-ups get tempted by a shiny game that promises: *"Give me a little money and I'll make you rich TODAY — skip the whole line!"*

It sounds amazing. But here's the honest truth: for most people, that game doesn't work.

## Why the flashy game fools people

When someone wins big, they tell *everybody*. When someone loses, they usually stay quiet. So it *looks* like winning is easy — but that's only because we mostly hear from the winners. Grown-ups call this a **survivorship trap**: you see the survivors, not the many who didn't make it.

The games grown-ups actually play are called things like **day trading**, **crypto**, and **prediction markets**. They can go up or down super fast. A few people do get lucky. But most people who try to get rich quick end up with *less* than they started with.

## The honest lesson

Skipping the line feels exciting, but it usually doesn't work. Two rules keep you safe:

- 🐢 **Keep saving slowly.** Boring money that grows a little every year really adds up.
- 🤏 **Only risk a tiny bit.** If you ever try a risky game, use just a small amount you can totally afford to lose — never your treehouse money.

Being priced out of a treehouse is a real, unfair problem — it's not your fault, and wanting a shortcut makes sense. But the shortcut that promises "rich today" is the one that empties the most pockets. Slow and steady, with only a tiny bit of risk, keeps you in the game. 🌱
