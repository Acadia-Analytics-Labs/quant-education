# What's a Lemonade Stand Really Worth? 🍋

Say you build a lemonade stand. It makes a little money every summer. One day a friend says, "I'll buy your stand from you — how much is it worth?"

Here's the tricky part. The stand isn't worth just the coins in the cash box today. It's worth **all the money it will ever make in the future**, added up. Next summer's profit, and the summer after that, and the one after that.

But there's a catch, and it's the whole secret of grown-up money.

## Why a future dollar is worth less

A dollar today beats a dollar next year, and it's not just because you're impatient. If you had the dollar now, you could put it in the bank and it would grow a little. So next year's dollar has some catching up to do — which means, looking back from today, it's worth a bit less than a full dollar.

The further away the money is, the more we shrink it:

- A dollar you'll get **next year** might be worth about **95 cents** today.
- A dollar **ten summers** from now might be worth only about **60 cents** today.

This shrinking has a fancy name: **discounting**. It just means "make future money smaller so we can compare it to money today."

## Adding up the future

To find what the lemonade stand is worth, you:

1. Guess how much money it makes **each future summer**.
2. **Shrink** each future summer's money down to today's value.
3. **Add** all those shrunk numbers together.

That total is the stand's value *right now*. Grown-ups do the exact same thing to figure out what a whole company — like the one that makes your favorite phone — is worth. They just use a spreadsheet instead of a lemonade jug. 🍋🏆
