# Ride the Wave, Don't Guess It 🏄

Meet **Maya**. She loves surfing. But here's the thing about the ocean: nobody can tell which tiny ripple will grow into a giant wave, and which one will just fizzle out. Guessing is a great way to get dunked.

So Maya doesn't guess. She has a simple rule: **wait until a wave is clearly big and rolling — then hop on and ride it for as long as it keeps going.**

## The big idea in one sentence

> **You can't predict the top — so follow the wave, and let a few huge rides pay for all the tiny wipeouts.**

This is exactly how some famous traders think. In 1983 a trader named Richard Dennis took a bunch of beginners — even a game designer! — and taught them a few simple rules. People called them the **Turtles**. The rules were basically Maya's rules for the ocean, but for prices:

- 🌊 Wait until a price is *clearly* going up before you buy. Don't guess the bottom.
- 💦 If it turns against you, get out fast and cheap. Small loss, no drama.
- 🏆 If it keeps climbing, hold on and ride it a long way.

## Why lots of tiny losses are totally okay

Here's the surprising part: **most of the time, the wave fizzles and Maya takes a small tumble.** She loses more often than she wins! But her wipeouts are tiny and her big rides are HUGE. So it all adds up in her favor.
